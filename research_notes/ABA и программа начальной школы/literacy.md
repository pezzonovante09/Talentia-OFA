# Early literacy and reading for autistic children aged ~5–10: ABA and related evidence-based methods

Scope note: notes collected 2026-11-17 from web search plus a few page fetches (about 22 tool calls). Several full texts (Springer, SAGE) were paywalled or blocked, so some effect sizes come from abstracts, publisher summaries or secondary summaries; this is marked where it applies. Design labels used: **SCD** = single-case design, **RCT** = randomized controlled trial, **QED** = quasi-experimental, **SR/MA** = systematic review / meta-analysis.

## 1. What do systematic reviews and meta-analyses say? Which components have the strongest evidence?

### Takeaway
Every major review reaches the same conclusion: autistic children benefit from the same explicit, systematic instruction that works for other struggling readers, especially phonics/letter–sound teaching and prompting-based sight-word teaching. The evidence base, however, is small, mostly SCD, and often rated low quality. The most robust single practice for word recognition in children with ASD plus moderate/severe ID is **time delay** (prompting). At the practice level, NCAEP 2020 rates **Direct Instruction, prompting, video modeling, peer-based instruction and self-management** as evidence-based for academic/pre-academic outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Whalon, Al Otaiba & Delano (2009), *Focus on Autism and Other Developmental Disabilities* 24(1):3–16. SR of 11 studies, 61 participants aged 4–17, organized by the five components of reading. Conclusion: children with ASD "can benefit from reading instruction consistent with reading research", but the research is "still preliminary". — [PMC2808040](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2808040); [Reading Rockets summary](https://www.readingrockets.org/research-by-topic/evidence-based-reading-instruction-individuals-autism-spectrum-disorders)
- Of the 11 studies in that review, only 4 evaluated methods for improving text comprehension (as characterized by Knight & Sartini, 2014). — [PMC2808040 / core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/235421892.pdf)
- El Zein, Solis, Vaughn & McCulley (2014), *JADD*, doi 10.1007/s10803-013-1989-2. Synthesis of K–12 reading comprehension studies from 1980–2012: 9 SCD, 1 QED and 2 single-group studies. Four studies used strategy instruction (question generation, graphic organizers, prediction). Most used researcher-made probes; only 2 used standardized measures. Conclusion: interventions that improve comprehension for students with reading difficulties "may improve reading comprehension" in students with ASD. I could not retrieve its effect sizes. — [Meadows Center](https://meadowscenter.org/library/reading-comprehension-interventions-for-students-with-autism-spectrum-disorders-a-synthesis-of-research/)
- Bailey & Arciuli (2019/2020), "Reading Instruction for Children with ASD: A Systematic Review and Quality Analysis", *Review Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*. Studies reported gains in phonics, reading accuracy, fluency and/or comprehension, but "few were of adequate or strong quality". — [UTS OPUS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/142665); [doi 10.1007/s40489-019-00185-8](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s40489-019-00185-8)
- A review of time delay for teaching word and picture recognition to students with severe developmental disabilities (1975–2007, 30 experiments, scored with SCD quality indicators) concluded that time delay is an evidence-based practice for picture and sight-word recognition by Horner et al. (2005) standards. This is Browder, Ahlgrim-Delzell, Spooner, Mims & Baker (2009), *Exceptional Children* 75:343–364. The authorship is confirmed through a reference list; I saw only an abstract snippet. — [SAGE record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001440290907500305); [reference list](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/07434618.2018.1470668)
- An earlier meta-analysis exists: Browder & Xin (1998), sight-word research for individuals with moderate and severe disabilities. Not retrieved. — [reference list](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/07434618.2018.1470668)
- A 2011 evaluation of sight-word instruction for students with autism found that students learned to identify printed words, including those with limited oral language and no prior reading instruction. However, "no studies addressed the effects of sight word instruction on broad literacy outcomes." — [BSL record](https://antoniusacademie.mijn.bsl.nl/sight-word-instruction-for-students-with-autism-an-evaluation-of/551636); [Springer s10803-010-1165-x](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10803-010-1165-x)
- NCAEP 2020 (Steinbrenner et al.) narrowed ~60,000 articles (1990–2017) to nearly 1,000 that met quality standards. It reports notable growth in studies successfully targeting academic/pre-academic skills. Video modeling is classed as an EBP for pre-academic/academic outcomes. Direct Instruction moved up from "some evidence" in the prior review to a full EBP for academic skills. Prompting, peer-based instruction, social narratives and self-management are also listed for academics. — [FPG news](https://fpg.unc.edu/news/fpg-review-30-years-autism-research-points-new-best-practices-and-trends); [NCAEP executive summary](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/sites/ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/files/imce/documents/EBP%20Executive%20Summary.pdf); [VKC tip sheet](https://tipsheets.vkcsites.org/evidence-based-practices-for-autistic-students-for-educators/); [NCAEP domain matrix](https://afirm.fpg.unc.edu/sites/afirm.fpg.unc.edu/files/imce/documents/NCAEP%20Domain%20Matrix%20%28Steinbrenner%202020%29.pdf)
- Dos Santos et al. (2024), *CoDAS*, MA with GRADE on phonological awareness interventions in ASD. Shared reading and software activities with words/phrases can change phonological awareness. One pre–post pooled gain on the TOPEL was 6.21 (95% CI 3.75–8.67, I² = 0%). The authors call for larger samples and better intervention description. — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/codas/a/7D5GTZkJQLv37bJZS3w5LWS/?lang=en)
- Boyle et al. (2019), *Focus on Autism and Other Developmental Disabilities* 34. MA of 11 shared-reading interventions: positive effects on listening comprehension and expressive communication. — [Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-shared-reading-on-the-early-language-and-literacy-skil/)
- A 2025 *JADD* MA pooled 5 SCD studies (K–12) of visual-support reading comprehension interventions ("PGR"; I could not expand the acronym because the full text was blocked). Overall moderate-to-strong effect, Tau-U = 0.85. Technology-delivered versions were more variable than paper-based ones. — [Springer s10803-025-07014-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-025-07014-4) (abstract via search snippet only)
- A 2025 *LSHSS* MA examined *correlations* between emergent literacy skills and later word recognition and comprehension in young autistic children. It is not an intervention study. — [ASHA 2025_LSHSS-24-00108](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_LSHSS-24-00108)
- An older review of technology-based academic interventions (1993–2012) advised caution when teaching academics to individuals with ASD through technology alone. — [record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c629t-22c35224465a1dcad9d2dca773b0cdc962081b303d1d7217b5d99606d6e8857a3)

### Inferences
- Component ranking by evidence strength (my synthesis, not from any single review):
  1. Prompting with time delay for sight-word and picture-word recognition: many SCD studies, rated EBP.
  2. Explicit phonics / letter–sound correspondence inside a structured curriculum: an RCT (ELSB), reviews, and cross-language support.
  3. Shared reading for listening comprehension and phonological awareness: MA, small N.
  4. Comprehension strategy instruction and visual supports: SCD only.
  5. Technology-only delivery: weakest and most variable.
- For an app, this argues for (a) systematic prompting with fading built into each trial, (b) letter–sound content, not only whole words, and (c) an adult in the loop. Software-only gains in ASD studies consistently relied on extra adult support (see question 2).

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for El Zein et al. (2014) and the 2021 SCD comprehension meta-analysis were not retrievable.
- I did not verify the NCAEP classification of Technology-Aided Instruction and Intervention specifically for academic outcomes.
- I did not retrieve National Standards Project Phase 2 (2015) literacy entries.
- I did not find Knight/Spooner/Browder's later reviews (e.g., Spooner et al. on comprehension for students with severe disabilities) with numbers.

## 2. Phonics vs sight-word approaches; named programs (ELSB, Edmark, Reading Mastery/DI, Headsprout, ABRACADABRA)

### Takeaway
Sight-word teaching reliably produces recognition of the taught words, including in non-speaking children, but it does not generalize to untaught words. Phonics (sub-lexical) instruction is what produces generalization. The strongest controlled evidence is the ELSB RCT, in which phonics instruction beat Edmark (a sight-word curriculum). Headsprout, Reading Mastery/Corrective Reading and ABRACADABRA all show gains in ASD samples, but in small or preliminary studies that typically needed additional adult support.

### Cited Findings
- **ELSB vs Edmark (RCT).** The WWC review rated the trial as meeting standards *without reservations* (randomized, low attrition). Students had severe developmental disabilities, with an autism subgroup reported. ELSB was delivered for a full school year; the comparison group received Edmark.
  - Nonverbal Literacy Assessment, Phonic Skills: 67.66 (ELSB) vs 52.86 (Edmark), significant.
  - Conventions of Reading: 21.25 vs 18.31, favoring ELSB.
  - PPVT-III vocabulary: not significant.
  - Source: [WWC study 95152](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/95152)
- The publisher's autism-specific report gives phonics d = 1.50 for ELSB vs d = 0.59 for the comparison group. This is a **vendor source** and should be weighted accordingly. — [ELSB research PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0559/9294/2762/files/ELSB_Research_for_Students_with_Autism_and_Students_with_Severe_Developmental_Disabilities.pdf)
- An IES-funded RCT of ELSB in general-education classrooms (pairs randomized within pairs) was planned. I found no published results. — [IES award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/implementing-emergent-literacy-program-students-intellectual-disabilities-and-autism-general)
- **Generalization (cross-language review).** Phonology-to-symbol interventions improved reading and spelling across alphabetic, syllabary and logosyllabic systems. Generalization to untrained words was reported **only when sub-lexical phonology was the focus**. Massed whole-word practice with prompting and differential reinforcement improved reading of the taught words. — [ASHA Evidence Map summary of Bailey & Arciuli 2022, *Autism* 26(2):389–405](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/3e2e6049-086a-ee11-8147-005056834e2b)
- **Edmark.** A 2021 study compared table-top and computer-assisted delivery of Edmark with students with ASD for one school semester. Outcomes were not retrieved. — [University of Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/the-edmark-reading-program-a-comparison-of-computerized-and-table/)
- **Reading Mastery (QED).** Kamps et al. (2016), *J Dev Phys Disabil* 28(5):703–722, compared small-group Reading Mastery with peers (n = 32) against typical reading instruction (n = 30) for "moderate to high functioning" young children with ASD, measuring beginning literacy. Outcome numbers were not retrieved. — [record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........bac0e3292bdd10285309349f3686af69); [Springer](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10882-016-9503-3)
- **Corrective Reading / DI comprehension (SCD).** Flores & Ganz (2007) used DI (Corrective Reading components) to improve comprehension in children with autism and developmental delays. Head, Flores & Shippen (2018) used a DI comprehension program with students with ASD or developmental delay. — [Scribd: Ganz 2007](https://www.scribd.com/document/659094437/Ganz2007); [record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-e227t-4c1ba8b5d1d7664127b5c874ad0da314b00703dcc81362b04487d5cb34534d53)
- **Headsprout Early Reading.**
  - Grindle et al. (2013): 4 students with autism gained between 14 months and 3 years of Word Recognition Age after about 14 weeks. The students needed substantial extra teacher support to progress through the episodes. — [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/429568/1/Herring_et_al_accepted_180319.docx)
  - A series of SCDs with 6 students with ASD identified the adaptations needed, because the program was built for typically developing children. — [Springer s10864-015-9244-x](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10864-015-9244-x)
  - A parent-delivered RCT (n = 26 children with ASD vs treatment as usual) measured reading and print motivation. — [PMC8586403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586403)
  - A 2023 feasibility RCT (n = 16, ages 7–10.9) met all feasibility benchmarks; in-person and telehealth delivery were both feasible. Efficacy is preliminary only. — [Springer s10864-023-09529-1](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10864-023-09529-1); [trial registry](https://ctv.veeva.com/study/headsprout-reading-program-in-children-with-autism-spectrum-disorder-and-reading-delay)
  - Headsprout Reading Comprehension: a multiple-case series with 6 children with autism showed increased comprehension, and practical support strategies were needed. — [Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/teaching-children-with-autism-reading-comprehension-skills-using-/)
- **ABRACADABRA (free, phonics-based).** A 2019 QED with 23 children with autism found gains in word and passage reading accuracy. — [Behaviorist Book Club summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/efficacy-of-abracadabra-literacy-instruction-in-a-school-setting-for-c/). An Arciuli-team feasibility trial with SLP delivery (including telepractice) found significant gains for the intervention conditions over control. — [ASHA LSHSS 2020](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/2020_LSHSS-20-00019)
- **Preschool (WWC).** In a separate WWC review of preschool emergent-literacy interventions for autism (phonological awareness lessons plus Road to the Code vs business as usual), the phonological awareness result was not significant. — [WWC study 95059](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/95059)
- **Russian practice literature.** Practitioner sources say many children with ASD find whole-word ("global") reading easier than letter sounds, and recommend limiting it to meaningful words. They also recommend combining whole-word reading with letters and syllable reading over the long term. — [practitioner PDF, school 69](https://sch69sz.mskobr.ru/attach_files/obucheniya-chteniyu-detey-s-ras.pdf) (descriptive, not empirical)

### Inferences
- A combined approach is best supported:
  - a small set of meaningful sight words for quick early success and for functional/AAC use;
  - **systematic letter–sound work from the start**, because only sub-lexical teaching generalizes.
- A sight-word-only track (Edmark-style) underperformed phonics in the only RCT head-to-head (ELSB).
- Commercial software programs (Headsprout, ABRACADABRA) work in ASD only with adult support and adaptations. This is consistent with an "adult present" app mode.

### Gaps
- Outcome data were not retrieved for Kamps et al. 2016, the Edmark CAI vs TTI study, or the Headsprout parent RCT.
- I found no head-to-head RCT of phonics vs sight words restricted to autistic children aged 5–10.

## 3. How literacy is taught with ABA techniques (DTT, errorless learning, prompting/time delay, MTS, stimulus equivalence, selection responses)

### Takeaway
The ABA toolkit for early reading is well established at the SCD level:
- **constant/progressive time delay** and **simultaneous prompting** (near-errorless) for sight words;
- **matching-to-sample (MTS)** conditional discriminations (dictated word → printed word, picture ↔ printed word), which produce untaught (emergent) relations, including oral reading, in some children;
- **selection/pointing responses** in place of oral reading for non-speaking children.

Effects are consistent but vary across individuals, and samples are tiny.

### Cited Findings
- Time delay is an EBP for picture and sight-word recognition in students with severe developmental disabilities (30 experiments, 1975–2007). — [Browder et al. 2009, Exceptional Children](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001440290907500305)
- A comparison of constant time delay (CTD) vs simultaneous prompting (SP) for functional sight words with 4 students aged 8–11 with moderate ID or ID+ASD found both effective; the study compared their efficiency (SCD). — [U Kentucky](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-constant-time-delay-and-simultaneous-prompting-proc/)
- **Complex auditory-visual samples (SCD).** 6 children with autism were trained with dictated word + picture samples and printed-word comparisons. All showed class-consistent (equivalence) performance after training. — [PMC2831442](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2831442); [secondary summary of Groskreutz et al. 2010](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/aba-fundamentals/using-complex-auditory-visual-samples-to-produce-emergent-relations-in/)
- **Multiple-sample MTS (SCD, Brazil, 4 students with autism).** Two needed few trials to reach criterion and showed emergent untaught relations. Emergence was only partial, and maintenance of dictated-word → printed-word was weak. — [UFSCar repository](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/handle/20.500.14289/9907)
- Gomes (Brazilian journal): 3 participants with autism learned the arbitrary relations, named printed words, and showed derived relations for some stimuli (SCD). — [REBAC](https://periodicos.ufpa.br/index.php/rebac/article/download/1975/3801)
- **Japanese study (3 children with autism).** Emergent matching of novel printed words to pictures and vice versa, plus emergent oral reading and spelling (generalized reading). — [Tsukuba repository, RASD 7(2)](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/29103/files/RASD_7-2.pdf)
- After conditional-discrimination training within activity schedules, participants responded to printed words by doing the depicted task, matched words to pictures, and read the words without explicit training (SCD). — [PMC2741070](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2741070)
- Students with limited oral language and no prior reading instruction learned to identify printed words through sight-word instruction. — [BSL / JADD 2011](https://antoniusacademie.mijn.bsl.nl/sight-word-instruction-for-students-with-autism-an-evaluation-of/551636)
- A cross-language SR found that massed practice with or without prompting, plus differential reinforcement, improved whole-word reading. — [ASHA Evidence Map, Bailey & Arciuli 2022](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/3e2e6049-086a-ee11-8147-005056834e2b)
- **Phonics added to ABA (SCD, n = 1).** One minimally verbal adolescent with ID received intensive phonics within ABA. Early reading comprehension emerged, measured as word/picture matching and following written instructions (Nicolosi et al. 2024, via a third-party summary). — [Behaviorist Book Club](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/the-effect-of-phonics-skills-intervention-on-early-reading-comprehensi/)
- **Selection responses in phonics teaching.** Adapted letter–sound correspondence instruction for learners aged 9–18 with ASD and complex communication needs (Caron, Light & McNaughton) showed that "LSC progress can still be made at an older age with systematic instruction". — [Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-adapted-letter-sound-correspondence-instruction-with-o/)
- **Assessment problem.** It is hard to assess phonological awareness in AAC users because most tests require spoken responses. — [AAC journal 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2022.2121226); [Penn State AAC](https://aac.psu.edu/?p=4731)
- **AAC users with ID (SCD, 7 children aged 6–10).** The children acquired phonological awareness and decoding through systematic, evidence-based materials (summary page). — [Behaviorist Book Club](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/reading-instruction-for-students-with-intellectual-disabilities-who-re/)

### Inferences
- An app trial structure of "hear a word / see a picture → select the printed word from 2–4 choices" is a direct implementation of the auditory-visual MTS procedures above. Testing the untaught directions (printed word → picture; naming) is how equivalence-based programs check for generalization, and it is cheap to build as probe trials.
- CTD (0-second delay, then a fixed delay) and SP fit a tap-to-select UI naturally. Both are near-errorless and suit children who become frustrated with errors.
- Phonological awareness and phonics can be taught and assessed with **selection responses**: tap the picture that starts with /m/, tap the letter for this sound, tap the word you hear. This makes them accessible to non-speaking children. That an app should do this is my inference, but it is consistent with the ALL/Light & McNaughton line of work.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analytic effect size specific to stimulus-equivalence reading programs in ASD.
- I did not retrieve the ALL (Accessible Literacy Learning; Light & McNaughton) efficacy data.
- Studies on errorless learning versus least-to-most prompting specifically for reading in ASD were not found in this pass.

## 4. Recommended sequence of prerequisite and early literacy skills

### Takeaway
No single empirically validated sequence exists for ASD. The sequence used by structured curricula (ELSB) and ABA programs is supported piecemeal:
1. identical matching (letter/word to letter/word);
2. receptive identification of letters ("touch M");
3. letter–sound correspondence;
4. first-sound and phonological awareness via picture selection;
5. blending/segmenting;
6. decoding simple words (CVC in English; open syllables in Russian);
7. word–picture matching for comprehension;
8. sentence- and text-level comprehension with visual supports.

A small set of meaningful sight words is usually taught in parallel.

### Cited Findings
- The ELSB outcome measure (Nonverbal Literacy Assessment) splits early literacy into phonic skills, conventions of reading and vocabulary. This reflects a curriculum built on phonological awareness and phonics with nonverbal response options. — [WWC study 95152](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/95152)
- Phonological awareness is described as the single largest predictor of reading outcomes, and reading instruction for AAC users should build phonological awareness and decoding. — [AAC journal 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2022.2121226)
- A 2025 MA links emergent literacy skills in young autistic children to later word recognition and comprehension (correlational). — [ASHA LSHSS 2025](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2025_LSHSS-24-00108)
- Early comprehension in the phonics + ABA single case was measured as word–picture matching, then following written instructions. — [Behaviorist Book Club, Nicolosi et al.](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/the-effect-of-phonics-skills-intervention-on-early-reading-comprehensi/)
- The Russian «Мастерская речи» method (group, older preschool / early primary children with ASD) combines letter work with reading syllables and words and matching them to pictures. — [FRC MGPPU PDF](https://autism-frc.ru/system/articles/files/000/001/567/original/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%8F_%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%87%D0%B8.pdf?1687296924)
- Russian practitioner guidance: start with words that are meaningful to the child, and combine global reading with letters and syllables. — [school 69 PDF](https://sch69sz.mskobr.ru/attach_files/obucheniya-chteniyu-detey-s-ras.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible app sequence (my synthesis, to be confirmed by the partner center's specialist):
  1. Identical matching: letter ↔ letter, then word ↔ word.
  2. Receptive letter identification by name or sound: hear, then tap.
  3. First-sound identification by picture selection.
  4. Syllable assembly (Russian: consonant + vowel open syllables МА, МУ).
  5. Blending syllables into short words.
  6. Printed word ↔ picture matching, in both directions as probes.
  7. Phrase or instruction ↔ picture.
- Each step should use MTS with 2 → 3 → 4 choices and prompt fading.
- The sequence is a prerequisite-logic ordering, not a validated ladder. The partner ABA specialist should confirm it, which is consistent with the project rule that the ABA spec is not changed without a live specialist.

### Gaps
- I found no study that compared alternative skill orders for ASD.
- No data were found on mastery criteria specific to literacy targets, such as how many sessions at 80–90% correct before advancing.

## 5. Reading comprehension difficulties specific to ASD (hyperlexia, inference) and supports

### Takeaway
A common ASD profile is adequate or even precocious decoding with impaired comprehension, rooted in oral language and listening comprehension (the Simple View of Reading). Hyperlexia affects a notable minority of autistic children (estimates around 6–20/21%, from secondary citation). Supports with evidence: explicit strategy instruction, DI comprehension programs, graphic organizers and visual supports (SCD, Tau-U ≈ 0.85), and shared reading for listening comprehension.

### Cited Findings
- Nation, Clarke, Wright & Williams (2006), *JADD*, doi 10.1007/s10803-006-0130-1. Group study, 41 children with ASD. Word reading, nonword reading and text accuracy were mostly in the average range, but comprehension was impaired.
  - Some children read accurately with very poor comprehension (hyperlexic profile).
  - Others were poor at both word and nonword reading.
  - Others could read words but not decode nonwords.
  - Interpreted within the Simple View of Reading.
  - Exact subgroup counts were inconsistent in the snippets and are not reported here.
  - Sources: [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/6517/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10803-006-0130-1)
- A later analysis describes a "hyperlexic/poor comprehender" group whose difficulties covered **listening** comprehension as well as reading comprehension. — [UCL manuscript](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10067122/1/autism%20accepted.pdf)
- Ostrolenk et al. (2017), *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 79:134–149. SR of hyperlexia: a hyperlexic-like profile characterizes "a substantial portion" of the autism spectrum, and 84% of published hyperlexia case studies were autistic. The prevalence figure is given as 6–20% in one secondary source and 6–21% in another; I could not verify it in the abstract. — [PubMed 28478182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28478182/); [Psychology Today](https://psychologytoday.com/au/blog/read-like-a-psychologist/202308/hyperlexia-precocious-reading-or-reading-disorder); [INSAR 2018 abstract](https://insar.confex.com/insar/2018/webprogram/Paper28551.html)
- Comprehension interventions in the El Zein et al. synthesis included question generation, graphic organizers and prediction. — [Meadows Center](https://meadowscenter.org/library/reading-comprehension-interventions-for-students-with-autism-spectrum-disorders-a-synthesis-of-research/)
- Visual-support comprehension interventions: Tau-U = 0.85 across 5 SCD studies; technology-delivered versions were more variable. — [JADD 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-025-07014-4)
- Shared reading improves listening comprehension in children with ASD (MA of 11 studies). — [Boyle et al. 2019](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/effects-of-shared-reading-on-the-early-language-and-literacy-skil/)
- DI comprehension programs (Corrective Reading) improved comprehension in SCDs with children with autism or developmental delay. — [Ganz 2007](https://www.scribd.com/document/659094437/Ganz2007); [Head, Flores & Shippen 2018](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-e227t-4c1ba8b5d1d7664127b5c874ad0da314b00703dcc81362b04487d5cb34534d53)
- Russian practitioner warning: a child can be taught syllable reading and still not understand what is read (mechanical reading). The guidance calls for keeping meaning central. — [school 69 PDF](https://sch69sz.mskobr.ru/attach_files/obucheniya-chteniyu-detey-s-ras.pdf)

### Inferences
- An app should not treat correct decoding as mastery. Every decoded word should be checked for meaning, for example by a word → picture selection probe. This guards against the hyperlexic profile, where accurate reading masks poor understanding.
- Inferential questions ("why", "how does he feel") are the weakest area and should come late, with pictures as answer options.

### Gaps
- No study was found that is specific to inference instruction for autistic children aged 5–10.
- Exact hyperlexia prevalence and the Nation et al. subgroup sizes were not verified from the primary texts.

## 6. Evidence for literacy in Russian (Cyrillic, syllable reading) or other transparent orthographies with ASD

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed empirical (controlled or SCD) study** of teaching reading in Russian to children with ASD. Russian sources are descriptive and methodological, mainly from the Federal Resource Center for ASD (MGPPU, «Рассвет» school) in the journal «Аутизм и нарушения развития». International evidence on non-English languages, including transparent alphabetic Turkish and French, supports phonics (sub-lexical) instruction, and generalization appears only when sub-lexical units are taught. That finding transfers in principle to Russian's fairly transparent, syllable-based decoding.

### Cited Findings
- Bailey & Arciuli (2022), *Autism* 26(2):389–405. SR of literacy instruction for autistic children speaking languages other than English. The ASHA map counts 19 studies; the authors' university news piece says 29, a discrepancy I could not resolve.
  - Languages: Japanese (Katakana syllabary and Kanji), French, Swedish, Turkish, Chinese. Russian/Cyrillic was **not** mentioned.
  - For alphabetic orthographies, phonics was associated with better word reading and spelling.
  - Phonology-to-symbol teaching at phoneme, **syllable**, morpheme or whole-word level improved reading and spelling.
  - Generalization occurred only with sub-lexical focus.
  - Limitations: small samples and unconfirmed diagnoses.
  - Sources: [ASHA Evidence Map](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/3e2e6049-086a-ee11-8147-005056834e2b); [Flinders news](https://blogs.flinders.edu.au/caring-futures-institute/2021/08/31/direct-literacy-instruction-beneficial-for-children-with-autism-around-the-world/)
- Nikonova & Pavlova (2020), «Обучение чтению детей с РАС», «Аутизм и нарушения развития» 18(2):70–76, doi 10.17759/autdd.2020180210. Presents adapted materials for teaching reading to older preschool and early primary children with ASD at the «Рассвет» school (FRC MGPPU) and describes typical groups of deficits that hinder learning to read. The article is **descriptive**; no outcome data were seen, because the PDF could not be text-extracted. — [psyjournals (EN page)](https://psyjournals.ru/en/journals/autdd/archive/2020_n2/nikonova_pavlova); [PDF](https://psyjournals.ru:443/journals/autdd/archive/2020_n2/autdd_2020_n2_nikonova_pavlova.pdf)
- «Мастерская речи» (FRC MGPPU, 2022): group method for preschool and early-primary children with ASD that combines letters, syllable and word reading, and word–picture matching. — [FRC PDF](https://autism-frc.ru/system/articles/files/000/001/567/original/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%8F_%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%87%D0%B8.pdf?1687296924); [Nikonova et al. 2022 PDF](https://psyjournals.ru:443/journals/autdd/archive/2022_n4/autdd_2022_n4_Nikonova_et_al.pdf)
- The «Личный букварь» ("personal primer") method for speaking children with ASD uses the child's cognitive and emotional profile plus visual support (same journal; descriptive). — [Tikhonova 2018 PDF](https://psyjournals.ru:443/journals/autdd/archive/2018_n2/autdd_2018_n2_tikhonova.pdf)
- Russian practice sources recommend combining global (whole-word) reading with letters and syllable reading. They warn against mechanical syllable reading without comprehension. — [school 69 PDF](https://sch69sz.mskobr.ru/attach_files/obucheniya-chteniyu-detey-s-ras.pdf); [SGU article](https://old.sgu.ru/sites/default/files/textdocsfiles/2020/10/21/564-572str.pdf)
- Brazilian Portuguese (a relatively transparent orthography) MTS/equivalence studies with autistic children show emergent reading relations (SCD). — [UFSCar](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/handle/20.500.14289/9907); [SciELO RBEE](https://scielo.br/j/rbee/a/FhpphVB8jMs679PrxkgwTjj/?lang=en)

### Inferences
- For Russian, the international finding (sub-lexical teaching generalizes, whole-word teaching does not) favors syllable-based decoding (СГ open syllables → words), with a small meaningful sight-word set and with meaning checked through picture matching.
- Kyrgyz is also Cyrillic and fairly transparent, so the same logic would apply. However, I found no ASD literacy data for Kyrgyz.
- Because Russian evidence is practice-based only, any Russian-specific sequence should be presented to the partner center's specialist as a proposal, not as evidence-based.

### Gaps
- No empirical Russian- or Kyrgyz-language ASD reading intervention studies were found. eLibrary and CyberLeninka were not reached directly by search, so a targeted search there may still turn up dissertations or small studies.
- The full text of Nikonova & Pavlova (2020) could not be extracted, so its outcome claims, if any, are unknown.
- The Bailey & Arciuli 2022 study count (19 vs 29) is unresolved.
