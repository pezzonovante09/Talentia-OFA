# Tablet/app-based and computer-assisted instruction (CAI / TAII) for academic and pre-academic skills in autistic children (~5–10): evidence, apps, design features

Scope note: about 18 searches/fetches. Most claims come from abstracts, registries and secondary summaries (behavioristbookclub.com, news releases). Only one primary full text was read: Pellecchia et al. 2020, PMC7541454. Effect sizes and moderator claims marked "secondary" should be checked against the original paper before they are quoted in a product decision.

---

## 1. NCAEP 2020 TAII and systematic reviews/meta-analyses of tablet/iPad/CAI for ASD academic skills

### Takeaway
TAII is one of 28 evidence-based practices in NCAEP 2020. Meta-analyses of single-case studies show large within-child effects for tablet/CAI academic instruction. Group-design evidence is weaker: the pooled effect is medium (d≈0.47, wide CI), and the strongest independent RCT of a commercial ABA-style CAI package (TeachTown) found no benefit. Overall, technology works as a delivery vehicle for well-built instruction, but it does not stand in for the instruction itself.

### Cited Findings
- NCAEP 2020 (Steinbrenner et al.) lists **Technology-aided instruction and intervention (TAII)** as one of 28 EBPs. It updates the earlier NPDC review, covers research from 1990–2017, and classified 972 articles into the 28 categories — [NCAEP Executive Summary](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/sites/ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/files/imce/documents/EBP%20Executive%20Summary.pdf); [Texas Autism Resource: TAII](https://txautism.net/interventions/technology-aided-instruction-and-intervention)
- The NCAEP definition of TAII: instruction or intervention in which technology is the central feature and is specifically designed or employed to support learning or performance of a skill/behaviour. It combines the technology with instructional procedures for using it or applying it in context — [CAPTAIN TAII training doc](https://captain.ca.gov/documents/ebptrainings/ebpt-taii-TechnologyAidedInstructionIntervention.pdf); [Texas Autism Resource](https://txautism.net/interventions/technology-aided-instruction-and-intervention)
- Boundary: AAC that uses technology (e.g., speech-generating apps) is counted under the **AAC** EBP, not TAII. Technology can be embedded in most other EBPs, so TAII is a categorisation choice — [Texas Autism Resource](https://txautism.net/interventions/technology-aided-instruction-and-intervention)
- Barton et al. 2017 (Remedial and Special Education) meta-analysis: **CAI**, a sub-category of TAII, met evidence-based criteria across both single-case and group studies — [Barton 2017 record](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1177/0741932517729508)
- **Grynszpan et al. 2014** (*Autism* 18(4):346–361), meta-analysis of "innovative technology-based interventions" (computer programs, interactive DVDs, shared active surfaces, VR; no robotics). Controlled studies gave an overall **d = 0.47 (95% CI 0.08–0.86)**, a similar effect in the 10 RCTs, and **no moderation by age or IQ**. **Intervention duration was negatively correlated with effect size** — [PubMed 24092843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092843/); [ASHA Evidence Map summary](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/beb37c61-affa-4436-9ae7-568f8c95d992)
- **Knight, McKissick & Saunders 2013** (JADD), review of technology-based academic instruction for ASD, applying strict single-case quality standards: only **3 of 29 studies** met them, so the evidence was judged insufficient at that time — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/a-review-of-technology-based-interventions-to-teach-academic-skills-to/); [Ledbetter-Cho 2018 context](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10803-018-3573-2)
- **Ramdoss et al. 2011** (two systematic reviews): (a) communication, J Behav Educ: CBI "should not yet be considered a research-based approach" for communication but is "promising"; (b) literacy, RASD: mixed results, with some large effects and some null studies. Participant heterogeneity made it impossible to identify which CBI variables drive effectiveness. The combined search yielded 16 studies and 119 participants — [Texas State repository](https://digital.library.txst.edu/items/9f29951a-4e22-469a-9b12-2f4ba251a5ca); [IMFAR 2011 abstract](https://imfar.confex.com/imfar/2011/webprogram/Paper9016.html); [ERIC EJ917104 record](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/eric__EJ917104)
- **Hong et al. 2017**: meta-analysis of single-case research on tablet-mediated interventions for ASD (all skill types, not only academics), using the Tau nonoverlap measure — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/a-meta-analysis-of-single-case-research-on-the-use-of-tablet-mediated/)
- **Ledbetter-Cho et al. 2018** (JADD 48:3021–3036): meta-analysis of **19 single-case studies** (2000–2017) of touch-screen devices targeting academic skills/engagement. A secondary summary reports that **learner-operated tablets gave larger gains than teacher-operated** ones and that age/functioning level did not moderate outcomes. The abstract snippet, however, says "no moderating variables related to intervention characteristics were identified". The two accounts conflict — [JADD record](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10803-018-3573-2); [secondary summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/meta-analysis-of-tablet-mediated-interventions-for-teaching-academic-s/)
- 2019 Review Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders, iPad-specific academic meta-analysis: **7 single-case studies**, Tau-U + HLM. No differences by subject, grade or number of sessions. Introducing the iPad intervention produced a significant change — [Univ. of Kentucky record](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/measuring-the-academic-outcomes-of-ipads-for-students-with-autism/)
- Math-specific synthesis (Liu et al., 2024, per search summary): 27 studies (2012–2022) on autism and/or ID, **Tau-U = 0.98** for mathematics, no significant moderators. The search did not pin down which result held this paper, so the attribution is unverified — [GCSU repository record](https://kb.gcsu.edu/fac-staff/1016)
- **Valencia, Rusu, Quiñones & Jamet 2019** (*Sensors* 19(20):4485): systematic review of **94 studies** on technology in educational settings for ASD, covering skills, UX/usability/accessibility, and game elements. The conclusion is optimistic: virtual agents, AI, VR and AR are presented as promising for a "comfortable" learning setting. It is an HCI-oriented review, not an effect-size synthesis — [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390%2Fs19204485); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/6934889047bd4fc1b86f51e2da063059)

### Inferences
- The positive verdict rests mostly on **single-case designs** with small n, typically discrete academic targets such as sight words, number identification and matching, with an adult running the procedure. That matches the ABA "with an adult" model, not unsupervised edutainment.
- In Grynszpan, effect size fell as intervention duration grew. That is consistent with novelty effects and with the TOBY/TeachTown pattern of usage dropping off (see §2/§5). Short, targeted programmes look safer than open-ended ones.
- Design labels: single-case meta-analyses report Tau-U/nonoverlap, which is **not comparable** to group d. Do not compare "Tau-U 0.98" with "d 0.47" as if they measured the same thing.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full NCAEP 2020 report, so I lack the exact TAII study count and age-band breakdown (whether 6–11 is covered).
- The Odom et al. technology reviews (e.g., Odom et al. 2015, JADD, TAII for adolescents) were not retrieved.
- The Ledbetter-Cho learner- vs teacher-operated moderator remains unresolved without the full text.

## 2. Specific apps/programs with published evaluations

### Takeaway
Only a handful of commercial or academic programmes have controlled trials in autistic children, and the results are sobering. **TeachTown:Basics** gave positive results in a small developer-led RCT and null results in a large independent RCT, with signs of harm through displacement of teaching. **Headsprout** has one small positive parent-mediated RCT plus single-case data, and heavy adult support was needed. **ABRACADABRA** online had a null RCT. **TOBY** and **FindMe** had null primary outcomes. **MITA** rests on large observational studies run by the developer. Most consumer "autism apps" (Otsimo, AutiSpark, Lookee, Leeloo) have no peer-reviewed efficacy studies that I could find.

### Cited Findings
**TeachTown:Basics (ABA-based CAI: discrete trials on computer + off-computer lessons)**
- **Whalen et al. 2010** (*Autism*): LA preschool/kindergarten classes, n=47 (22 treatment, 25 control), 3 months, 20 min on computer + 20 min matched off-computer activities. The TeachTown group improved more on language and cognitive measures — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/efficacy-of-teachtown--basics-computer-assisted-intervention-for-the-i/); [eSchool News](https://www.eschoolnews.com/innovative-teaching/2009/11/23/computer-based-learning-improves-performance-of-autistic-children-in-los-angeles-united-school-district/)
- **Commercial bias:** first author Christina Whalen is a TeachTown co-founder — [eSchool News](https://www.eschoolnews.com/2009/12/07/software-helps-students-with-autism/?print)
- **Pellecchia et al. 2019** (JAACAP), independent RCT in Philadelphia K–2 autism support classrooms (59 teachers, n=154 students). There were **no significant differences** vs usual care in language or cognitive outcomes. Students who used TeachTown **more** had **worse** language outcomes than controls — [PubMed 31128269](https://www.pubmed.gov/31128269/); [Pellecchia 2020, PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)
- **Pellecchia et al. 2020** (*Autism*, implementation follow-up; 73 teachers, 58 schools). The software targets nonverbal cognitive ability equivalent to ages 2–7 and recommends 20 min/day on software + 20–30 min/day of off-line lessons. Control teachers' DTT use rose ~154% and PRT use ~377% over 8 months. TeachTown teachers' DTT and PRT use fell slightly. Teachers said they treated TeachTown as a **replacement** for one-to-one instruction rather than a supplement, citing easy data collection, appeal to students and parents, coverage during staffing shortages, and behaviour management. The district **de-implemented** TeachTown afterwards — [PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)

**Headsprout Early Reading (behaviour-analytic online phonics)**
- **Nally et al. 2021**, RCT with parents delivering Headsprout at home, plus behavioural skills training, an online support group and consultation; n=26 children with ASD. Gains over control in reading rate, word and non-word reading, and target sounds/words — [PMC8586403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586403)
- **Thompson et al. 2022**, single-case (AB/ABC/multiple baseline), 6 autistic students. All progressed, but **"extensive additional teacher support"** was needed to get through the episodes — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/school-classroom/beyond-tier-three--individualized-levels-of-support-during-headsprout/)
- A University of Nebraska trial (NCT03055533; planned n=16, 12 weeks) is listed as completed. I found no published results — [trial record](https://ctv.veeva.com/study/headsprout-reading-program-in-children-with-autism-spectrum-disorder-and-reading-delay)

**ABRACADABRA (free web literacy programme, Concordia/Flinders)**
- **Bailey, Sellwood, Rillotta, Raghavendra & Arciuli 2022** (*Res Dev Disabil*), RCT: 21 children (ages reported as 5–12 or 6–10), **16 h** of online ABRACADABRA via telepractice (COVID) + parent-led shared book reading. **No reading gains.** Parents liked and used the programme. The authors call for more intensive/longer instruction and better uptake of home book reading. An earlier, smaller, in-person ABRACADABRA trial by the same group had reported benefits — [PMC9482337](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9482337); [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/service-delivery/a-trial-of-online-abracadabra-literacy-instruction-with-supplementary/); [Flinders project page](https://www.flinders.edu.au/caring-futures-institute/our-research/research-projects/literacy-children-asd)

**TOBY (Therapy Outcomes By You), iPad, parent-mediated, early-learning curriculum**
- **Whitehouse et al. 2017** (JCPP 58(9):1042–1052), RCT: n=80, TOBY (≥20 min/day) + TAU vs TAU, 6 months. **No group difference on the primary outcome (ATEC)** at 3 or 6 months. Some secondary gains, e.g., understanding single words. Usage averaged **19 min/day in months 1–3 and 2 min/day in months 4–6**. 70% of carers rated it "a helpful therapy planning tool". The authors frame it as an inexpensive **add-on**, not a replacement — [Telethon Kids summary](https://donate.telethonkids.org.au/our-research/reports-and-findings/2017/September-2017/a-randomised-controlled-trial-of-an-ipad-based/); [The Kids news](https://www.thekids.org.au/news--events/news-and-events-nav/2017/june/children-with-autism-may-benefit-from-apps/)

**FindMe (iPad social-attention game; context, not academic)**
- **Fletcher-Watson et al. 2016** (*Autism*), RCT: n=54 children under 6, ~2 months of iPad use vs waitlist. **No group differences** on parent-report or parent-child play. Children were engaged and parents rated it highly. Later eye-tracking work found within-app and some "distant generalisation" image effects in typically developing children — [PMC5015758](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5015758); [DART Edinburgh project page](https://dart.ed.ac.uk/research/click-east/)

**MITA (Mental Imagery Therapy for Autism / "Language Therapy for Kids – MITA")**
- Observational only. One registered study (NCT02708290) compared 3,540 MITA users with 5,226 treatment-as-usual children (ages 2–12), matched by propensity score. A medRxiv preprint reports 1.7× receptive and 1.4× expressive language gains vs controls. Other reports give ~3× or 2.2× (n=6,454). **The figures differ across versions.** Authors including Vyshedskiy and Dunn are partners in ImagiRation, the developer. The studies were self-funded, and the authors themselves say an RCT is warranted — [medRxiv preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.10.19014159v4.full.pdf+html); [MedicalResearch interview](https://medicalresearch.com/autism-prefrontal-synthesis-training-can-enhance-language-acquisition/)
- An earlier bioRxiv report followed **1,514** young children using the parent-administered app for 4–12 months. Older children and those with milder ASD progressed faster. This is uncontrolled and developer-run — [bioRxiv 146449](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2017/06/08/146449.full.pdf)
- A new observational registry study (NCT07299175) plans n=150 with outcomes at baseline, 3 and 6 months (ATEC, Mental Synthesis checklist) — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07299175](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07299175)
- Commercial model: free download, Premium $69.99/yr — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1020290425)

**Other tablet apps (academic research prototypes)**
- Esposito et al. 2017: three tablet apps (attention, vocabulary, imitation) for children with ASD reproduced effective educational training — [Tor Vergata PDF](https://art.torvergata.it/bitstream/2108/195040/1/esposito2017.pdf)
- 2024 Android arithmetic app with autism-specific adaptations, validated with only 5 students — [Blanco et al. 2024, Univ. Cantabria](https://matematicasyautismo.unican.es/doc/blanco2024design.pdf)

**Consumer "autism apps": Otsimo, AutiSpark, Lookee, Leeloo**
- **No independent peer-reviewed efficacy studies were found.** AutiSpark's store listing claims its games were "tested and approved by special educators and occupational therapists". That is a self-claim, and an award (Nappa 2025) is not an outcome study — [App Store AutiSpark](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1498731271); [Nappa Awards](https://www.nappaawards.com/?p=20746)
- Otsimo is described in press and blogs as developed with child psychologists and as gamifying ABA. I found no outcome study — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/otsimo-expands-autism-education-app-us); [Yu-kai Chou blog](https://yukaichou.com/education-gamification/top-ten-apps-to-help-improve-the-lives-of-autism-spectrum-children/)
- Leeloo appears only as an AAC app in store listings, and nothing at all was found for Lookee — [Educational App Store list](https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/best-autism-apps-ipad-android)

### Inferences
- A consistent pattern runs across TeachTown 2010→2019 and ABRACADABRA in-person→online. **Small developer-led trials are positive; larger independent or lower-support trials are null.** Commercial bias and "dose without adult" both look like plausible explanations.
- The TeachTown displacement finding matters most for product design. An app that makes life easier for the adult can **reduce** effective adult-delivered teaching. A design that keeps the adult in the loop, e.g. an app that structures the adult's trial rather than replacing it, guards against this.
- Usage decay is large: TOBY fell from 19 to 2 min/day, and Grynszpan found a negative duration–effect correlation. Claims that a programme works need actual-use logs.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluations were found for **GEMIIS, Cognitive Toybox, Lookee, Leeloo, Otsimo, AutiSpark**. Searches for "Cognitive Toybox" returned only unrelated tablet-assessment games, so its existence and evidence base are unverified.
- **Proloquo2Go** (AAC context) was not searched in this pass. NCAEP files tech-AAC under AAC, not TAII.
- I have no primary text for Nally 2021 effect sizes or the Bailey 2022 age range (reported inconsistently as 5–12 and 6–10).

## 3. Design features with evidence (errorless/prompting, consistency, distractors, reinforcement, voice vs text, touch formats, data collection, adaptivity)

### Takeaway
Few studies isolate a single design feature experimentally. The rationale for CAI rests on consistent stimulus presentation, removing extraneous stimuli, immediate feedback, and learner control. These are mostly theoretical or practitioner claims, plus within-study package effects. The field lacks an evidence-based method for designing and evaluating such tools, and poor design can produce null results that get blamed on "technology".

### Cited Findings
- Fletcher-Watson (2014, Review J Autism Dev Disord) reviewed technology for autism and found **no consistent, evidence-based method for designing, implementing and evaluating** such tools. Poorly designed programs can yield weak learning outcomes that are misattributed to the approach itself — [Springer s40489-013-0003-4](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40489-013-0003-4)
- Rationale for **minimal distractors**: many autistic learners have difficulty filtering sensory input, so a computer that presents only the necessary information may help focus attention (Sansosti et al. single-subject meta-analysis, as summarised; attribution via search summary) — [Springer s40489-014-0042-5](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40489-014-0042-5.pdf)
- **Consistent presentation** is cited as an advantage of CAI over variable human delivery. The same practitioner source stresses testing whether skills **generalise beyond the screen** — [Behaviorist Book Club summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/computer-technology-for-autistic-students/)
- **Immediate feedback and learner control** are recommended in Panyan's 1984 review. This is a secondary summary and older evidence — [Behaviorist Book Club](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/computer-technology-for-autistic-students/)
- **Built-in ABA mechanics**: TeachTown's computer lessons use a discrete-trial format with built-in reinforcement and prompting. Packaging ABA procedures into software did not by itself produce outcomes in the independent RCT — [PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)
- **Learner-operated vs adult-operated tablets**: possibly larger gains when the learner operates the device. This comes from a secondary summary of Ledbetter-Cho 2018 and conflicts with the abstract's "no moderators" statement — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/meta-analysis-of-tablet-mediated-interventions-for-teaching-academic-s/); [JADD](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s10803-018-3573-2)
- **Adult support remains necessary even with a well-sequenced behaviour-analytic programme**: Headsprout single-case work needed "extensive additional teacher support" — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/school-classroom/beyond-tier-three--individualized-levels-of-support-during-headsprout/)
- **Data collection** was a key reason teachers liked TeachTown, and it was part of why they used it to replace rather than supplement instruction — [PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)
- **Co-design** with educators, clinicians and autistic users is recommended so content and accessibility fit learners — [Nature Index topic summary](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/technology-enhanced-instruction-for-autism-spectrum-disorders)
- In the gamification meta-analysis (§4), the most common design elements were feedback, rewards, custom learning, monitoring and personalisation, each in about 40–50% of studies — [HKMU record](https://scholars.hkmu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-use-of-gamified-interventions-to-enhance-social-interaction-a/)

### Inferences
- The best-supported design stance is to build the app as a **faithful carrier of an ABA procedure** (discrete trial, prompt hierarchy, reinforcement), with the adult staying in the loop. A procedurally faithful app alone is not sufficient, as TeachTown and Headsprout show.
- Data collection is a double-edged feature. It helps clinicians, but it also makes the app look like a complete programme, which encourages replacement behaviour in adults. A logging design that shows the adult what to do off-screen may reduce this.

### Gaps
- I found **no experimental comparisons** of voice versus text instructions, touch-response formats (tap vs drag), reinforcement schedules inside apps, errorless versus trial-and-error procedures within apps, or adaptive versus fixed difficulty for autistic children aged 5–10. Each needs a targeted search, e.g. "stimulus fading app autism", "auditory prompt iPad autism single-case", "drag vs tap young children".
- Note: NCAEP lists prompting, reinforcement and time delay as separate EBPs. The evidence for those procedures comes mostly from adult-delivered studies, and their transfer into apps is assumed rather than tested.

## 4. Gamification and game elements: engagement vs disrupted learning; screen time

### Takeaway
Reviews consistently report that game elements raise **engagement** in autistic children. The most common elements are feedback, rewards, personalisation and progress tracking. Evidence that they improve **learning or generalised outcomes** is thin and heterogeneous. Engagement is not efficacy: FindMe and TOBY engaged children and parents and still showed no primary-outcome benefit. I found no rigorous studies of the specific harms of points, timers, unpredictability or overstimulation.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 meta-analysis of gamified interventions for social interaction: **20 studies, 349 participants**, mostly children and adolescents. Common elements were feedback (50%), rewards (50%), custom learning (45%), monitoring (45%) and personalisation (40%). Two themes: boosting engagement and amplifying intervention outcomes — [HKMU record](https://scholars.hkmu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-use-of-gamified-interventions-to-enhance-social-interaction-a/)
- 2025 PRISMA review of **24 gamified AR studies**: interventions were largely effective for **engagement**, attributed to rewards, progress tracking and narrative. Caregivers were involved in implementation but not in co-design — [Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/research-on-fire-25/126025010)
- 2024 *Smart Learning Environments* systematic review and scientometric analysis of gameful approaches for autistic children's education proposes a research agenda for design, use and assessment. It indicates the field is immature — [SLE 2024](https://slejournal.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s40561-024-00309-6.pdf)
- A small 2025 review of 5 studies found gamification works when grounded in **visual structure, adaptive feedback and user-centred design**, but few studies address real-world implementation — [IJIP](https://ijip.in/articles/integration-of-gamification/)
- 2021 Frontiers in Psychology scoping review: a pragmatic practitioner guide to analogue and digital game-based interventions, explicitly not a meta-analysis — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.669734/pdf)
- Engagement without transfer: the FindMe app engaged children "regardless of developmental level" and was highly rated by parents, yet showed no real-world effect — [PMC5015758](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5015758)
- Appeal can be harmful. In TeachTown, "student and parent appeal" and help with "managing challenging behaviour" were reasons teachers let the app replace instruction, and higher use was associated with worse language outcomes — [PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)
- Novelty decay: TOBY use fell from 19 to 2 min/day — [Telethon Kids](https://donate.telethonkids.org.au/our-research/reports-and-findings/2017/September-2017/a-randomised-controlled-trial-of-an-ipad-based/). Effect size decreased with longer intervention duration — [PubMed 24092843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092843/)

### Inferences
- In the ABA context, game elements are best treated as **reinforcers whose value must be checked**, not as default decoration. Narrative, avatars and points help sustain attention, but their link to learning is unproven, and they can make the tablet itself the preferred activity. In TeachTown, that led adults to substitute screen time for teaching.
- For a "with an adult" mode, a reasonable conservative choice is short sessions, a predictable structure, and rewards tied to correct responses (contingent), with no ambient animation, timers or random events. This follows the minimal-distractor rationale in §3, not direct experimental evidence on gamification harms.

### Gaps
- I found **no controlled studies** isolating harms of timers, countdowns, unpredictable or random rewards, loot-style mechanics, avatars, or high-intensity audio-visual effects for autistic 5–10-year-olds.
- **Screen time** concerns specific to autistic children (e.g., association studies on screen exposure and outcomes, AAP guidance) were not searched in this pass.

## 5. Role of the adult/parent; parent-mediated technology; generalisation from screen to real world

### Takeaway
Across the trials found, the programmes with positive controlled results kept an adult heavily involved, e.g. Headsprout with parent BST, consultation and support. Programmes used mostly as stand-alone screen time (TOBY over time, online ABRACADABRA, FindMe, TeachTown as a replacement) showed null primary outcomes. Generalisation to real-world settings is the weakest link, and several RCTs explicitly failed to show it.

### Cited Findings
- **Headsprout + parents (Nally 2021)**: positive RCT. Parents received behavioural skills training, an online support group and a consultative model, so the adult was trained and supported, not just handed an app — [PMC8586403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8586403)
- **ABRACADABRA online + parent-led shared book reading (Bailey 2022)**: null result. The authors cite insufficient intensity and weak uptake of the home reading component — [PMC9482337](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9482337)
- **TOBY (parent-mediated iPad)**: null primary outcome. Usage collapsed after 3 months, but 70% of carers valued it as a **planning tool** for therapy. The app's value may lie in structuring the adult rather than in teaching the child directly — [Telethon Kids](https://donate.telethonkids.org.au/our-research/reports-and-findings/2017/September-2017/a-randomised-controlled-trial-of-an-ipad-based/)
- **TeachTown design intent**: computer lessons were supposed to be paired with **off-computer, teacher-delivered lessons** (20–30 min/day). In practice teachers used it to replace one-to-one teaching, and outcomes were null or negative — [PMC7541454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541454)
- **MITA** is designed as caregiver-delivered exercises. Its developers claim a supplement effect, but the evidence is observational and conflicted (see §2) — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.10.19014159v4.full.pdf+html)
- **Generalisation failures**: FindMe RCT showed no effect on parent-child play or parent-report social communication. The authors note the field generally lacks evidence for generalisation to novel settings and for long-term maintenance — [PMC5015758](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5015758)
- TOBY's primary outcome (ATEC, a broad real-world measure) did not differ between groups, while the near-transfer secondary (understanding single words) did — [Telethon Kids](https://donate.telethonkids.org.au/our-research/reports-and-findings/2017/September-2017/a-randomised-controlled-trial-of-an-ipad-based/)
- Practitioner guidance: test whether skills generalise beyond the screen rather than assume it — [Behaviorist Book Club](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/computer-technology-for-autistic-students/)
- Caregivers in gamified AR studies were involved in implementation, not in co-design — [Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/research-on-fire-25/126025010)

### Inferences
- Effects tend to be found on **near-transfer, proximal measures** (in-app targets, specific word knowledge) and missed on **distal, real-world measures**. An ABA app should plan explicit generalisation steps: off-screen probes with real objects, multiple exemplars, and different adults or settings. It should also record those probes instead of counting in-app accuracy as mastery.
- Parent training (BST) and ongoing support appear to be part of the active ingredient in the one positive parent-mediated RCT (Headsprout). "Give parents the app" is probably not enough.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve how the positive single-case academic studies programmed generalisation, e.g. whether they used multiple-exemplar training or worksheet/real-object probes. That would need full texts of Ledbetter-Cho 2018 or the individual studies.
- I found no study comparing the same app used with vs without an adult present for autistic 5–10-year-olds.

## 6. Data privacy/ethics for apps used by autistic children (brief)

### Takeaway
No autism-app-specific privacy audit was found. The broader evidence is bad: children's apps and health or mental-health apps commonly send identifiers to third-party analytics and advertising. A developmental-behavioural paediatrician has stopped recommending commercial autism apps, such as behaviour trackers and visual timers, partly over these concerns.

### Cited Findings
- 19 of 24 of the most popular Google Play health apps transmitted user data to at least one third party. Dozens of apps marketed to people with mental-health conditions, including autism, have privacy problems — [The Transmitter/Spectrum](https://www.thetransmitter.org/spectrum/mental-health-apps-highlight-need-for-regulation)
- Jenny Radesky (developmental-behavioural paediatrician) has stopped recommending commercial apps, including behaviour trackers and visual timers for autistic children, in clinical practice — [Psychiatric News](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2020.11a32)
- Preschoolers' apps (JAMA Pediatrics, 124 children): about two-thirds of apps collected and shared persistent identifiers with third-party databases, with higher rates in lower-income households — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20200908/Study-finds-some-children-to-be-at-greater-risk-of-privacy-violations-from-digital-platforms.aspx)
- Large-scale Android study: about 19% of children's apps (1,100) shared sensitive data with third-party services whose terms prohibit use in children's apps — [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/thousands-of-android-mobile-apps-improperly-track-children-study-says/2018/04)
- A scoping review notes children's apps may be among the worst for numbers of third-party trackers — [PMC9209675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209675)

### Inferences
- An offline-first app with no third-party SDKs, analytics or ads is an actual point of difference in this category, not a formality. Session-level ABA data (trial outcomes, prompt levels) is effectively health or behaviour data about a disabled child.
- Ethical design issue from §2/§4: an app that is appealing and logs data can displace effective human teaching (TeachTown). Communicating clearly to adults that the app does not replace the specialist is part of responsible design.

### Gaps
- No autism-specific app privacy audits were found. Regulatory specifics (COPPA, GDPR-K, Russian 152-FZ, Kyrgyz law) were out of scope for this pass.
